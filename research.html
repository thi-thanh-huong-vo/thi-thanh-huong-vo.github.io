Ga₂O₃ wide-bandgap semiconductor

Research vision

Turning the imperfections of Ga₂O₃ into function

Motivation

Gallium oxide is best known for power devices that block high voltages with low leakage, and for solar-blind UV detection. Working with Ga₂O₃ devices, I have come to see a wider potential. The intrinsic defects that are usually treated as a problem to suppress also give the material a rich response to light and radiation. My long-term goal is to understand these defects well enough to design with them, rather than against them.

Research directions

Optoelectronic synapses

Defect states inside the wide bandgap allow Ga₂O₃ devices to respond to light far below the bandgap energy, including visible light, and to hold that response over time. With the right device design, this memory-like behavior could be tuned to mimic synaptic functions, opening a path toward light-driven neuromorphic hardware on a robust wide-bandgap platform.

Soft X-ray detection through electrical signatures

Conventional X-ray detectors typically rely on high photon energies and thick absorbing layers. My work shows that Ga₂O₃ devices carry characteristic features in their electrical response, such as the shift of a minimum-current point, that can distinguish soft X-rays at low dose. I want to develop this into a detection principle that works with thin devices and low radiation exposure, making X-ray sensing and imaging safer.

Approach: from characterization to junction design

Much of my work so far has used devices fabricated on commercial wafers. This let me focus on device physics, but it limited my control over the layer structure. My goal is to close this loop: design the junction with TCAD simulation, fabricate it, verify it through electrical and optoelectronic characterization, and feed the results back into the design. I will focus on two-terminal devices, such as Schottky and heterojunction diodes, whose simple structure makes them well suited to scalable arrays for both imaging and neuromorphic applications.

Building on my work

  • Direct probing of trap dynamics Measuring how traps capture and release charge: the basis for defect-based functions.
  • Reverse low-bias conduction mechanisms How defects shape leakage and transport at the junction.
  • Charge neutral point shift under soft X-ray The electrical signature behind low-dose soft X-ray discrimination.
  • X-ray detection of stacked A4 sheets and soft X-ray shielding of wood films Toward low-dose imaging and material discrimination.
  • Experimental and modeled C-V from 300 to 673 K Linking TCAD simulation with measurement: the starting point for junction design.

Expected impact

Together, these directions aim to extend Ga₂O₃ beyond power switching toward devices that sense, remember and image, while turning the junction itself into a designable element rather than a fixed starting point.
