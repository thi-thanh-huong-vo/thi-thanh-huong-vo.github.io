About me

I grew up in Vietnam wanting to understand how things work, and that question has taken me from a physics classroom in Ho Chi Minh City to semiconductor research abroad. I started as a physics teacher, then chose to move on my own and begin again as a researcher. My work explores how gallium oxide devices behave under heat, electrical stress and radiation. I learn fast, work hard, and rarely stop at the first answer, because I want to become an engineer whose work ends up in real devices.

Outside the lab, I read to nourish my mind and the way I connect with people, travel to see the world through other eyes, and cook because it teaches me to take care of myself.

Research

My work connects experiment and TCAD simulation to understand how Ga₂O₃ Schottky barrier diodes behave under heat, pulsed bias and radiation. I study capacitance–voltage behavior from 300 to 673 K, reverse low-bias conduction and trap dynamics with pulsed current and single-voltage-pulse methods, and apply Ga₂O₃ diodes and thin films as sensitive X-ray detectors.

Keywords: wide-bandgap semiconductors, reliability, electrical and optoelectronic characterization.
